\section{LimbuOCR: An End-to-End Page-to-Book OCR System for Limbu, and
a Pipeline Toward the Nepal Language
Pack}\label{limbuocr-an-end-to-end-page-to-book-ocr-system-for-limbu-and-a-pipeline-toward-the-nepal-language-pack}

\textbf{Ashish Thapa} Ampixa Labs \texttt{ashish@ampixa.com}

\begin{multicols}{2}

\subsection{Abstract}\label{abstract}

Nepal's languages are written in more than a dozen scripts (Limbu in
Sirijonga, Newar in Prachalit, Santali in Ol Chiki, among others), yet
almost none has optical character recognition good enough to turn a
scanned newspaper or book into editable text, so their printed record
stays trapped in page images. The obstacles are concrete: tiny training
corpora, legacy fonts that store the wrong bytes, no rights-cleared
benchmarks, and multi-column pages that defeat naive reading-order
recovery. \textbf{LimbuOCR} is a deployed system that begins to close
that gap for Limbu. It turns a scanned page, image batch, or PDF into
Unicode text with page and line structure, reading order, and crops that
lead back to every prediction; a script router and a 50-language
registry extend the same path to further recognizers as their data and
rights mature.

The Sirijonga recognizer is trained from 4,872 real-print line pairs
across 16 newspaper editions, each crop and label taken from the same
PDF and split by publication date. On the held-out silver test it
reaches 0.474\% codepoint character error rate (CER), 0.979\% grapheme
CER, and 85.92\% exact-line accuracy; on complete held-out pages the
full pipeline reaches 2.575\% page CER with 96.85\% line-detection F1.

Beyond Limbu, the same source-pairing workflow has produced real-print
silver labels for Newar, Tirhuta, Ol Chiki, Sunuwar, Kirat Rai, and
Gurung Khema (in several cases the first such data of any scale), staged
training sets, a working Newar diagnostic recognizer, and two
mechanism-level negative results that redirected those routes. These are
engineering baselines against machine-converted text, not human-graded
accuracy: native review, a wider independent page set, and genuine
Devanagari-written Limbu evaluation remain open.

\subsection{1. Introduction}\label{introduction}

The practical test for this project is straightforward. Someone should
be able to hand LimbuOCR a scanned book, photographed pages, or a PDF
and receive editable Unicode text. When the system is unsure, the editor
should be able to find the source line and correct it without
reconstructing the OCR run. Meeting that test requires much more than a
low error rate on pre-cropped lines: the output has to retain page
boundaries, reading order, line boxes, crops, confidence, script
decisions, and source identity, and weak detections must remain visible
for review.

LimbuOCR now covers that path. It accepts PDFs and image batches,
detects lines, routes Sirijonga and Devanagari crops, restores reading
order, and writes editable and structured outputs. Reviewer decisions
remain tied to the source crops, and only accepted corrections can enter
a later training export. We evaluate the Sirijonga recognizer both on
cropped lines and through the full page path, so detection and ordering
errors remain part of the result.

One part of the data pipeline needs careful separation from the runtime
system. Some newspaper PDFs display Sirijonga with Namdhinggo-family
legacy fonts. Their stored character codes can be mapped to Unicode and
aligned with line images rendered from the same page. This gives us
scarce real-print training material, but it is a way of constructing
evidence, not the inference method: the deployed recognizer starts from
pixels and does not read a PDF text layer.

Limbu also previews the problems a broader OCR effort in Nepal will
face. It appears in two visually different writing systems; available
sources mix Unicode, legacy encodings, and cross-script transcription;
and its newspaper pages add multiple columns and page furniture under
low-resource constraints. We have parameterized the reusable
parts---detection, routing, assembly, export, and review---through a
50-language registry and run the same source workflow across a wider
newspaper archive. Section 10 reports what that produced and which
artifacts are deliberately not yet used. The measured subject of this
paper remains Limbu.

The paper makes five contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{An operational Limbu book-OCR research prototype} connecting
  page/PDF intake, preparation, detection, script routing, recognition,
  reading order, editable export, and review in one auditable system.
\item
  \textbf{A real-print Sirijonga data path with provenance}, recovering
  4,872 line pairs from 16 same-PDF page/text sources under a
  date-grouped, exact-label-leakage-filtered split.
\item
  \textbf{Line and page diagnostics on a date-disjoint silver test},
  comparing ft-v2 with the previous deployment on 902 lines and through
  complete held-out pages---including the demonstration that page CER is
  meaningful only under a declared serialization: the same predictions
  score 41.34\% or 4.329\% depending on the reading-order contract.
\item
  \textbf{A testable boundary for two-writing-system support}: a
  source-pixel audit rejects a mislabeled Devanagari OCR result and
  specifies the evidence a genuine Devanagari-written Limbu benchmark
  needs.
\item
  \textbf{A transferable language-pack pipeline}: the same pixel/text
  pairing, legacy-font conversion, and leakage-checked staging executed
  across the Nepal pack, yielding per-script real-print data, one
  diagnostic second-language recognizer, and two root-caused negative
  results, each reported with its current review status.
\end{enumerate}

Four research questions organize the work: whether same-PDF pixels and
legacy text layers can bootstrap Sirijonga recognition when
transcription is scarce (RQ1); which page components dominate end-to-end
error once line recognition is strong (RQ2); how a system should
distinguish source script, language, transcription encoding, and support
maturity (RQ3); and whether crop-linked review can reduce the time to a
corrected book (RQ4). The experiments answer RQ1 and RQ2 in preliminary
form, Section 8 answers RQ3 operationally, and RQ4 still needs a study
with fluent reviewers.

\subsection{2. Limbu OCR as a System
Problem}\label{limbu-ocr-as-a-system-problem}

Project sources use both Limbu and Yakthung for the language, and both
Sirijonga and Devanagari for its written form; Unicode assigns the Limbu
script U+1900--U+194F (Unicode Consortium 2025a). An OCR record
therefore needs at least three separate fields: the \textbf{language}
(Limbu), the \textbf{visual writing system} on the page (Sirijonga or
Devanagari), and the \textbf{transcription encoding} of the desired
output. A Sirijonga page paired with a Devanagari transcription is a
valid cross-script transcription task, but it says nothing about
recognizing Devanagari glyphs, and it changes the dictionary,
recognition head, fonts, and error analysis. Historical PDFs add one
more layer: a page may look like Sirijonga while its text layer stores
Latin or private codes rendered through an embedded font. A known font
map converts those codes into useful silver labels; the visible script
is still Sirijonga, and the deployed model still reads pixels.

We describe support with four levels---\textbf{runtime} (a model loads
and emits text), \textbf{operational} (the page/book workflow emits
reviewable artifacts), \textbf{measured} (quality is evaluated on
relevant real pixels), and \textbf{claim-grade} (frozen, rights-cleared,
human-reviewed evaluation). Sirijonga is measured on date-disjoint
silver diagnostics; the Devanagari route is merely operational; neither
is claim-grade. This vocabulary keeps ``supported'' from doing too much
work.

Two evaluation choices follow throughout: we score machine-converted
silver without calling it human ground truth, and we make source
grouping, leakage checks, normalization, missing predictions, and
reading order part of the metric definition rather than prose caveats.

\subsection{3. Related Work}\label{related-work}

Tesseract remains the reference example of segmentation and recognition
in a deployable engine (Smith 2007); LimbuOCR builds on PaddleOCR, whose
PP-OCR family combines lightweight backbones, sequence modeling, and
deployable recognition heads (Li et al. 2022; Cui et al. 2025). The
recognizer measured here is the PP-OCRv5 mobile model (PPLCNetV3
backbone, SVTR neck, CTC/NRTR heads); CTC enables sequence recognition
without character-level alignment (Graves et al. 2006) but does not
solve layout, which is why we keep line CER, detection F1, matched-line
recognition, and full-page CER separate. For low-resource scripts,
fonts, standardized text, rights, and representative evaluation
constrain progress at least as much as model size (Agarwal and
Anastasopoulos 2024); synthetic rendering and glyph-centered contrastive
learning broaden coverage (Sharma et al. 2024) without establishing
real-page performance, which matches our ablation experience (Section
8.1).

We report NFC codepoint CER and extended-grapheme-cluster CER under the
Unicode segmentation model (Unicode Consortium 2025b) because Limbu
combining signs split perceived characters across codepoints. Datasets
and models are treated as evidence objects in the sense of datasheets
(Gebru et al. 2021) and model cards (Mitchell et al. 2019).
Community-centered work on Yakthung digital heritage frames script
support as part of community-controlled tooling (Limbu 2024); our
focused search found no peer-reviewed public Sirijonga page-to-book
benchmark with comparable reporting, so we describe ft-v2 as an internal
silver baseline without a priority claim. The closest systems precedent
is the historical-document platform tradition of Transkribus (Mühlberger
et al. 2019) and postediting research on transparent, human-controlled
suggestions (Poncelas et al. 2020).

\subsection{4. The LimbuOCR Platform}\label{the-limbuocr-platform}

LimbuOCR separates model development from book processing. The offline
path turns audited sources and accepted corrections into datasets,
models, and evaluation records; the runtime path turns page pixels into
editable text and review tasks; human decisions, attached to their
crops, connect the two.

\end{multicols}

\begin{figure}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=1\linewidth,height=\textheight,keepaspectratio]{figures/system-architecture.png}
\caption{LimbuOCR system architecture. The upper path constructs and
validates evidence and model bundles; the lower path performs
page-to-book OCR. Review decisions remain attached to source crops and
can feed later audited training exports.}
\end{figure}

\begin{multicols}{2}

The runtime accepts a line crop, a full page, an ordered image set, or a
PDF rendered at a recorded resolution; capture tooling can deskew and
perspective-correct photographs while retaining raw and prepared image
hashes. PaddleOCR detects text on the full page, and each polygon
becomes a crop with page-relative geometry. The crop is the smallest
reviewable evidence unit: its record links source page, prepared page,
detector box, image, recognizer output, and final book line, so a missed
line or wrong character can be traced to the stage that produced it.
Recognition may be fixed to Sirijonga or Devanagari or chosen per crop;
the application bundles \texttt{limbu-sirijonga-ft-v2} (measured here)
and \texttt{deva-v2}, a generic Devanagari recognizer that supplies a
runtime route without any Limbu-specific accuracy result. Detected
regions are assembled by row or column---the six-column newspaper test
uses deterministic column-major order, recorded with every output and
score---and the book path writes \texttt{book.txt}, \texttt{book.json},
annotated pages, crops, Markdown projections, and scorer-compatible
document JSON. Weak lines, empty detections, suspected furniture, mixed
scripts, and suspicious vertical gaps enter review queues; corrections
fail closed against source and crop identity, and only explicitly
accepted text can reach a training export.

The same components are parameterized beyond Limbu. A shared
Unicode-block table identifies scripts, and a configuration-driven book
runner resolves each language's detector, recognizers, converters, and
writing-system profiles from a 50-language pipeline registry.
Registration does not grant permission to run: every recognizer is
marked \texttt{champion}, \texttt{diagnostic-only}, \texttt{blocked}, or
\texttt{failed-fit}, and an execution allowlist decides which statuses
may enter the production path, so a withdrawn or legally blocked model
stays visible but will not execute. Section 10 gives the evidence behind
those statuses.

\subsection{5. Building Real-Print Sirijonga
Evidence}\label{building-real-print-sirijonga-evidence}

\subsubsection{5.1 Source pairing and legacy-font
recovery}\label{source-pairing-and-legacy-font-recovery}

The real-print material comes from Limbu pages in the \emph{Naya Nepal}
supplement of Gorkhapatra epaper PDFs. Both sides of every pair come
from the same full PDF: we render the page image and extract the text
layer from that same page, which prevents pairing a thumbnail with
article text that was never laid out in it. Each retained row records
the PDF and its hash, page number, rendered page, line geometry, raw
codes, converted text, and crop. We manually checked the test page dated
2082-08-12: a six-column Sirijonga newspaper page whose rendered-image
SHA-256 is recorded in the source-verification report. Publisher pixels
are not reproduced here; the current owner determination covers derived
counts and metrics, while redistribution awaits a formal license
statement.

The pages display Sirijonga through Namdhinggo-family fonts, but their
text layers are not Unicode Limbu. During extraction we group PDF spans
into lines and convert the stored codes with \texttt{Limbu.map}, so the
Unicode label and its image crop trace back to one PDF layout object.
The pairing scales, but it is not gold ground truth: font maps can be
wrong, spans can arrive out of order or vanish, and grouping and
normalization can introduce errors. Every row is marked
\texttt{pdf\_text\_layer\_silver} and excluded from claim-grade
evidence.

\subsubsection{5.2 Grouped splitting and leakage
control}\label{grouped-splitting-and-leakage-control}

The rebuilt collection contains 4,872 non-empty line pairs from 16
publication dates. Every line from a date stays in one split: after
sorting the dates, indices congruent to 2 modulo 5 form the test split
(2082-03-24, 2082-08-12, 2082-12-25), indices congruent to 0 form
development, and the rest form training. Removing the 38 normalized
labels that repeat across split boundaries leaves 4,834 lines: 2,711
training (nine dates), 1,221 development (four dates), and 902 test
(three dates). Date grouping stops same-edition leakage and label
filtering catches repeated boilerplate; near-duplicate phrases and
shared page templates may remain.

\subsubsection{5.3 Training mixture}\label{training-mixture}

The ft-v2 training bundle has 46,753 training rows and 1,221 development
rows: 25,065 synthetic examples plus eight passes over the 2,711
real-print lines. Another 3,090 referenced synthetic images were lost in
a storage failure and excluded rather than regenerated under the old
names. Replay changes optimization weight, not sample independence.
Training used an NVIDIA L4 for 40 epochs (about 8.5 GPU-hours),
selecting epoch 39 on development; the bundle, YAML, log, checkpoint,
export, predictions, and scores survive, and building this paper checks
them without rerunning training.

\subsection{6. Model and Evaluation
Method}\label{model-and-evaluation-method}

The recognizer is initialized from PaddleOCR's pretrained PP-OCRv5
mobile checkpoint: PPLCNetV3 backbone at scale 0.95, SVTR neck, CTC/NRTR
heads, 48-by-320 inputs, at most 128 characters, trained 40 epochs with
Adam, a cosine schedule from \(10^{-4}\), one warm-up epoch, and batches
of 64. The output dictionary remains the working
\texttt{limbu-sirijonga-v2.txt}. The comparator is the preceding
real-heavy Sirijonga export; both models run through one local CPU
inference and scoring path on the same 902 images. ft-v2 never sees its
test dates during training or selection; the older model's history
cannot be isolated to the same standard, so this is an engineering
before/after comparison, not a controlled leaderboard.

Strings are normalized to NFC with whitespace preserved. CER is
Levenshtein distance divided by reference length, micro-averaged over
codepoints and over extended grapheme clusters; exact-line accuracy
counts predictions matching the entire normalized reference. A page run
starts from the complete rendered image: the detector proposes regions,
the recognizer reads each crop, low-value outputs are filtered, boxes
are ordered column-major, and a one-to-one matcher pairs predictions
with references at IoU of at least 0.5. We report detection
precision/recall/F1, CER over the fully assembled page text, CER and
exact accuracy over matched lines, and missed and unmatched counts.

The 16-page regression set catches deployment regressions, but 13 of its
dates occur in ft-v2 training or development, so we also rescore the
exported documents for the three line-test dates (907 reference lines,
date-disjoint from selection). We do not present page results as
accuracy claims: there is no human review, only three independent pages
against the project's minimum of 30, no permission yet to redistribute
pixels, and no complete multi-domain leakage audit. They are reported as
reproducible diagnostics.

\subsection{8. Lessons from the
Experiments}\label{lessons-from-the-experiments}

\subsubsection{8.1 Real-domain weighting beats indiscriminate
synthesis}\label{real-domain-weighting-beats-indiscriminate-synthesis}

Synthetic data supplied script and glyph coverage, but more of it did
not consistently help: one targeted synthetic/Unicode expansion worsened
the frozen real-print score, and expanding the dictionary during a warm
start replaced part of the recognition head and failed outright. ft-v2
worked after we kept the output space fixed and gave the aligned
real-print lines much more replay weight. Synthesis should be treated as
controlled coverage, ablated against a fixed real-domain test.

\subsubsection{8.2 Page CER is defined by its serialization
contract}\label{page-cer-is-defined-by-its-serialization-contract}

Full-page CER compares two long strings, so it measures sequence as much
as text, and the serialization rule is part of the metric definition. On
the 16-page set, the same recognizer output scores 41.34\% codepoint CER
when reference and prediction are linearized under different
rules---alongside 4,895 reference lines, 4,839 predictions, 4,700
geometric matches, 96.57\% detection F1, and only 2.66\% CER on matched
lines. Applying one explicit column-major linearization to both sides
resolves the contradiction: 4.329\% codepoint and 6.714\% grapheme CER
over the identical predictions, with no model retrained and no
recognized text altered. The mechanism is simple: on a six-column page,
small vertical offsets make a global y-sort weave columns together, and
edit distance counts the moved blocks as character errors. For RQ2, the
catastrophic-looking number was mainly an inconsistent sequence
definition; the remaining error belongs to recognition, missing and
extra lines, crop quality, and layout generalization. Every page score
now records its linearization, and the 41.34\% and 4.329\% figures must
never be read as two OCR systems.

\subsubsection{8.3 A target string does not identify the visual writing
system}\label{a-target-string-does-not-identify-the-visual-writing-system}

An early curriculum pack was labeled Devanagari-written Limbu because
its JSON references contain Devanagari Unicode. The PDF pixels, on
reinspection, show Sirijonga glyphs: the fonts include
\texttt{YakthungBold} and \texttt{NamdhinggoSILL}, and raw extraction
yields ASCII-like legacy codes. On the inspected page the 14-line
reference has 408 Devanagari-block codepoints and no Limbu-block
codepoints while the page itself is visibly Sirijonga---so the real task
is Sirijonga pixels to Devanagari transcription, not Devanagari OCR. A
surviving 31-crop diagnostic is therefore evidence about cross-script
transcription, and its lost scoring bundle is a second reason to exclude
it from the verified tables. Operationally (RQ3): manifests store
\texttt{language}, \texttt{visual\_script}, \texttt{source\_encoding},
and \texttt{transcription\_encoding} separately, \texttt{deva-v2} is
called a generic runtime route, and a Limbu-specific Devanagari result
will require visibly Devanagari pixels containing Limbu.

\subsubsection{8.4 Review burden is the next system
metric}\label{review-burden-is-the-next-system-metric}

None of the recognition scores says how long a book takes to correct.
Low average CER can coexist with missing headings, confidently wrong
lines, or an interface that forces the editor back to the source page
for every error. The RQ4 study should measure lines routed to review,
missing and extra lines, correction time per page and per thousand
characters, accuracy after bounded review, and the share of a book
needing resegmentation---review burden, rather than CER alone, decides
production usefulness.

\subsection{9. What Is Implemented}\label{what-is-implemented}

The repository contains a compact application (Line OCR, Paddle Page
OCR, and Paddle Book OCR, plus image-line fallbacks and diagnostic
probes) and the larger experiment workflow: capture manifests, page and
book outputs with source and crop hashes, triage and review queues,
fail-closed correction application, and reviewed-only training export. A
private CPU service runs the same application; superseded models and a
failed-fit manuscript-Newa export remain in the bundle with model cards
so their history stays inspectable. Model cards bind inference artifacts
and dictionaries by SHA-256, and the paper build verifies those
artifacts and code paths statically. What is \emph{not} yet
demonstrated: a complete human-reviewed book, a correction-time study,
and any Limbu-specific Devanagari validation.

\subsection{10. The Language Pack in
Pipeline}\label{the-language-pack-in-pipeline}

Sections 5--8 report Limbu experiments. Here we record what the same
workflow produced on the rest of the Nepal language pack: locate a
source document, classify its text layer (Unicode, legacy glyph
substitution, transcription, or none), build an explicit converter where
the font is understood, pair text with pixels from the same PDF object,
and check provenance and split leakage before staging a training set.
Training and quality claims wait for rights and native-label review;
most entries below have not cleared those gates.

\subsubsection{10.1 Substrate at scale}\label{substrate-at-scale}

Gorkhapatra's \emph{Naya Nepal} archive rotates pages for more than
fifty languages. Applying the Limbu same-page rule at two depths
retained \textbf{515,586} converted silver lines (monthly backfill,
March 2024--July 2025) and \textbf{306,971} lines (per-language rebuild)
across the 50-language registry; the collections overlap and are not
deduplicated, so their sum is not a corpus size. These labels identify
pages, not necessarily each line's language---Nepali furniture appears
on language pages---so line-level language identification must precede
per-language training. Gorkhapatra is Nepal's state-owned national
daily; an owner determination (2026-07-22) permits the derived counts
and metrics here, while page and crop redistribution awaits a formal
license statement.

\subsubsection{10.2 Newar, the second
language}\label{newar-the-second-language}

Newar (Nepal Bhasa) was chosen second because its Devanagari route could
exercise the full Limbu workflow quickly. A publication-date split
produced 4,096 training, 1,528 development, and 1,139 test lines; after
a storage failure, the recovered labels lack the row-level provenance to
reconstruct that isolation. On the frozen test, generic \texttt{deva-v2}
(never trained on Newar) records 6.223\% codepoint CER; warm-started
\texttt{newar-ft-v1} records \textbf{4.655\%} codepoint CER, 9.569\%
grapheme CER, and 24.50\% exact-line accuracy. The Limbu page path gives
19.92\% page codepoint CER, 91.16\% detection F1, and 9.05\%
matched-line codepoint CER on 19 pages, pointing toward recognition and
crop-domain error rather than reading order---though the evidence is too
compromised for a firm attribution. A later check found label whitespace
artifacts, 18 exact and 575 substring-level cross-split matches, missing
provenance, and an open rights review, so the pack is not used for
claims. The useful result is procedural: the shared path produced a
working second-language diagnostic within days, and the review then
stopped it from becoming an accuracy claim.

\subsubsection{10.3 Scripts with staged training, pending
review}\label{scripts-with-staged-training-pending-review}

Four other scripts have real-print material and staged training sets but
no training run; archives are hash-pinned, development splits follow
issue or page boundaries, and train/development intersections are zero.

\textbf{Tirhuta.} The Maithili e-zine \emph{Videha} publishes a parallel
Tirhuta edition (archived issues carry uploader-applied CC BY-SA 4.0
statements, recorded per issue), but its PDFs use legacy
glyph-substitution fonts. Recovering that mapping, including a 149-entry
conjunct extension, yielded \textbf{10,696} clean real lines---to our
knowledge the first real-print Tirhuta OCR substrate at this scale. The
staged split has 9,765 training and 844 development rows with a complete
issue held out, plus a 48,960-image synthetic pretraining pack in an
openly licensed font. No model is trained yet.

\textbf{Ol Chiki.} A magazine evaluation pack retains \textbf{772} clean
lines (of 895 candidates; a dark-pixel guard removed 123 blank renders).
The synthetic-trained v2 recognizer scores \textbf{8.26\%} codepoint CER
and 20.34\% exact-line accuracy on it, but we withdrew that model from
claim use after finding its training fonts came from the evaluation
edition. A public-source-only v3 training set (5,307 images: 242 real
train, 5,000 synthetic, 65 real development) awaits native review.
Santali OCR exists elsewhere; this is pack coverage, not a first-ever
claim.

\textbf{Sunuwar and Kirat Rai.} Government-hosted Sikkim Herald editions
yielded \textbf{9,743} clean Sunuwar/Jenticha and \textbf{3,699} Kirat
Rai lines through converters grounded in the relevant encoding proposals
with no unresolved units. The splits (8,422/514 and 2,865/193) remain
unused while legal review determines whether the State Portal permission
covers model training.

\subsubsection{10.4 Negative results that redirected
routes}\label{negative-results-that-redirected-routes}

The Lepcha converter returned no lines from 18 Sikkim Herald editions
because the article bodies are raster images with no live text
layer---no font converter can ever recover them. The Lepcha path now
starts from synthesis, anchored by a \textbf{132}-crop machine-labelled
diagnostic that is explicitly ineligible for claims. The manuscript-Newa
recognizer failed even on its training data for a structural reason: its
mobile backbone pools every sequence to 40 CTC timesteps while 95.7\% of
manuscript lines exceed 40 characters, and CTC cannot emit more labels
than timesteps, so no amount of data could fit. A T=200 configuration
passes shape and loss checks, and a leakage-filtered 1,961-row training
set with a sealed 635-line holdout awaits an approved run. Both failures
identify a mechanism and changed the next experiment.

\subsubsection{10.5 Source-first discipline for the hardest
scripts}\label{source-first-discipline-for-the-hardest-scripts}

For Gurung Khema and native-script Newar, work stops at the source
stage. Official Sikkim Herald Gurung Editions contain running Gurung
Khema print, overturning our earlier belief that only synthetic material
existed; a proposal-grounded converter can mechanically convert
\textbf{749} of 759 candidate lines, but none is used pending
provenance, native pixel-label review, and split checks. Newar itself is
modeled as four writing-system profiles (Prachalit, Bhujimol, Ranjana,
Devanagari): Bhujimol has no distinct Unicode encoding and Ranjana
remains unencoded, so neither can reuse the Prachalit route without an
explicit transcription policy. Bhujimol and Ranjana manuscript groups
were catalogued and acquired only from institutional archives with
explicit reuse statements, with every image hashed; there are still no
reviewed labels, models, or metrics. In this registry, ``supported'' can
mean only that input and output semantics are defined.

\subsubsection{10.6 What the pipeline does not
claim}\label{what-the-pipeline-does-not-claim}

No result in this section is claim-grade. The intended near-term system
has one Sirijonga recognizer, one shared Devanagari recognizer, and
per-script experts added only after rights-cleared, natively reviewed
evaluation exists. What exists today is the auditable
substrate---sources, converters, staged training sets, diagnostics, and
explicit refusals to run unreviewed models---and each remaining model
still has to earn its place per language, script, and domain slice.

\subsection{11. Research Agenda}\label{research-agenda}

The next work closes specific gaps, in dependency order. \textbf{First,
human-grounded Sirijonga evaluation}: fluent readers correct the three
date-separated line and page sets (keeping silver and corrected text,
with recorded disagreements), then the benchmark grows to at least 30
independent pages across newspapers and books, split by source; scoring
the same predictions against silver and corrected references will show
how much text-layer noise helps or hurts. \textbf{Second, genuine
Devanagari-written Limbu}: candidate pages must visibly use Devanagari,
contain Limbu, and carry references aligned to those pixels;
\texttt{deva-v2} becomes the baseline and routing is tested only after
both scripts have relevant page evidence. \textbf{Third, book and
phone-capture robustness}: skew, curvature, uneven light, damaged print,
varied fonts, and changing layouts, scored jointly with capture
preparation, on at least one complete work. \textbf{Fourth,
human-centered evaluation}: raw and post-review CER, missed and falsely
confident lines, share of lines routed to review, correction time per
page and per thousand characters, and reviewer agreement---queue
policies compared at fixed final quality, with accepted corrections
entering versioned training packs only after checks. \textbf{Fifth,
structure and fallback}: tables, figures, and captions with
structure-aware references; any large document model used for hard
regions must report trigger rate, latency, and measured gain.
\textbf{Sixth, gated transfer across the pack}: resolve the Sikkim
Herald legal question, obtain native review per staged set, then run the
approved chains (Tirhuta pretrain and fine-tune, Ol Chiki v3, Newa
T=200) against frozen, edition-disjoint tests---one language, script,
and domain slice at a time. This paper makes no claim-grade accuracy
statement for any language, including Limbu.

\subsection{12. Limitations and Rights}\label{limitations-and-rights}

\textbf{References and scope.} The largest numerical limitation is
reference quality: converted text layers carry encoding, ordering,
grouping, and normalization errors, and a model trained on one publisher
may learn its typography and PDF artifacts. Three held-out pages cannot
represent other books, publishers, fonts, periods, or cameras. The
previous deployment is an operational comparator, not a baseline suite;
external comparisons should wait for a frozen, human-reviewed image set.
Only Sirijonga has a surviving Limbu-specific result---the presence of a
Devanagari model is not evidence about Devanagari-written Limbu. Pack
numbers add script-specific caveats: many silver lines are
page-attributed rather than language-verified, the Newar pack has
cross-split leakage and missing provenance, the Ol Chiki diagnostic was
withdrawn over font overlap with its evaluation edition, and a pre-crash
per-language Devanagari scorecard lost its backing artifacts and is
omitted.

\textbf{Rights.} An owner determination (2026-07-22) for Gorkhapatra,
Nepal's state-owned national daily, permits the derived counts and
metrics reported here but not redistribution of pages or crops; the
paper uses artifact-derived diagrams only. Sikkim Herald training sets
remain unused while the State Portal permission is reviewed for training
use; only the Videha Tirhuta source has recorded per-issue CC BY-SA 4.0
statements. Any later release must state precisely which material its
rights basis covers.

\textbf{Community validation and reproducibility.} Metrics cannot settle
linguistic correctness, preferred script names, or community acceptance;
fluent Limbu readers need a standing role in benchmark review, error
taxonomy, and release decisions, with their corrections kept visible.
The paper build regenerates figures and checks source counts, splits,
metrics, model hashes, and runtime paths against local artifacts; it
does not repeat the L4 training run, whose bundle, checkpoint,
predictions, and scores remain available for inspection.

\subsection{13. Conclusion}\label{conclusion}

LimbuOCR is an operational research platform that turns page images and
PDFs into editable, structured, reviewable Limbu text. The measured
Sirijonga route records 0.474\% codepoint CER, 0.979\% grapheme CER, and
85.92\% exact-line accuracy on a three-date silver test, and 2.575\%
codepoint CER with 96.85\% detection F1 from complete pages. The same
source and release workflow has produced large silver collections,
converters, staged training sets, a Newar diagnostic, and two useful
negative results across the Nepal language pack. Those artifacts mark a
starting point: Sirijonga labels need native validation,
Devanagari-written Limbu needs verified pages of its own, and every
other language remains behind its rights and review gates. The
serialization correction and the withdrawn curriculum result show why
these boundaries belong in the method rather than a footnote. The next
meaningful measure is review burden on a complete book---how much time
the system saves while keeping missed text and uncertain lines visible.
